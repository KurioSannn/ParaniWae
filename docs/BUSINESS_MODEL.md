# Business Model - SILATARA

## 1. Value proposition

SILATARA offers a cultural learning journey that connects partner-approved heritage knowledge, beginner-friendly lessons, transparent AI practice feedback, and real-world school access. It also creates a digital channel for schools/instructors to present their teaching and attract interested learners.

## 2. Customer segments

### B2C
- curious beginners;
- current students/learners;
- cultural enthusiasts and diaspora (later, with localized content and verified partner permissions).

### B2B / institutional
- pencak silat schools/organizations;
- schools and universities using cultural-learning material;
- cultural institutions and educational programs.

### Supply-side partners
- instructors, masters, athletes, schools, and approved cultural knowledge holders.

## 3. Revenue hypotheses

Do not activate monetization before partner permission and willingness-to-pay validation.

1. **Freemium learning:** free cultural introduction and selected beginner materials; paid advanced/partner courses later.
2. **Premium instructor courses:** one-off course purchase or subscription with transparent revenue share.
3. **School SaaS:** school profile/content/class management and learner enquiry tools.
4. **Institutional licensing:** school/university/cultural institution learning access.
5. **Class lead/booking fee:** only if the school agrees and fees are transparent; don't charge for access to cultural knowledge without partner agreement.

## 4. Early pricing experiments

Do not invent final prices. Test willingness to pay using interviews and simple landing-page/pricing experiments. Consider local purchasing power and ensure partner share is explicit. For MVP, a no-payment pilot is acceptable if the business hypothesis is still being tested.

## 5. Key partnerships

- anchor pencak silat school/style partner;
- instructors/maestros who validate movement and content;
- additional verified schools for directory expansion;
- schools/universities for pilot learning;
- cultural/heritage institutions where relevant;
- technology providers only where data protection and reliability are appropriate.

Potential institutional names such as IPSI or a government office must not be presented as partners without formal agreement.

## 6. Cost structure

- product/engineering and maintenance;
- model inference and technical infrastructure;
- camera/video production and editing;
- instructor review/consultation fees;
- rights/consent and content governance;
- user support and partner onboarding;
- marketing/community activation;
- payment processing and security/compliance when applicable.

## 7. Benefit-sharing principles

- pay or otherwise compensate instructors for substantive content/review work where feasible;
- attribute content according to partner preference;
- agree commercial rights separately from educational permission;
- report usage/revenue to partners transparently;
- allow partners to revoke or restrict content according to agreement;
- do not use a school's name/logo to sell unrelated products.

## 8. Business validation experiments

| Hypothesis | Experiment | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| Beginners want a safe first step before visiting a school | Prototype test + follow-up interview | Users complete the learning loop and some request a real class. |
| Schools want a digital discovery channel | Partner interviews and a simple profile prototype | Partner supplies verified data and agrees to be listed. |
| Instructors will validate and maintain material | Paid/structured co-design pilot | Instructor completes reviews and accepts governance/benefit terms. |
| Users will pay for specialist lessons | Price interview/landing test | Actual purchase intent or small pilot conversion, not just “sounds good”. |
| Institutions may license learning content | 3-5 discovery conversations | Named budget owner, need, procurement path, and pilot request. |

## 10. Business risks

- partner may not want commercial use of their material;
- learner willingness to pay may be low for beginner content;
- directory may create low-quality leads unless partners verify schedule/contact;
- content operations and instructor review may scale more slowly than software;
- model performance may vary by device and movement;
- marketplace/booking can add operational complexity and is not MVP-critical.
