# Cultural Partner Playbook - Partner-Led Development

## 1. Why partner-led is the default

Pencak silat is culturally diverse. The platform cannot choose a single “correct” form for all traditions. The first movement curriculum must come from a credible school/instructor who agrees to co-design and validate the material. A partner relationship is not only a marketing logo: it is a cultural, pedagogical, rights, and safety dependency.

## 2. Partner selection criteria

Use this internal scorecard as a decision aid; it is not an official IPSI or BUDAYA GO! standard.

| Criterion | Suggested weight | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---:|---|
| Credibility and provenance of the tradition/school | 20% | History, recognized teachers, school documentation, local context. |
| Instructor availability and commitment | 20% | Named instructor, time available, participation in review sessions. |
| Willingness to validate movement rubrics | 20% | Written agreement to review demonstrations, phases, error taxonomy, and feedback. |
| Quality/availability of reference material | 15% | Permissioned video, reference camera views, terminology, movement notes. |
| Willingness to support beginner learning digitally | 15% | Co-design discussion, learner suitability, safety rules. |
| Ethics and safety commitment | 10% | Clear boundaries, consent process, content restrictions, child-safety practice. |

Do not select a school only because it is famous, nearby, or easy to contact. The best first partner is the one that can collaborate deeply and keep the scope valid.

## 3. Partner onboarding process

1. **Introductory meeting:** understand the tradition, school, teaching approach, and what may/may not be documented.
2. **Problem discovery:** ask instructors what beginners misunderstand, where they need physical correction, and which movements are safe to practice alone.
3. **Scope agreement:** choose one beginner curriculum and a small movement set (target 8-12; final number is partner decision).
4. **Rights and consent:** agree to recording, use, attribution, distribution, storage, revocation, and possible monetization.
5. **Reference capture:** record approved movement demonstrations with defined camera placement, lighting, framing, and phase description.
6. **Rubric co-design:** write assessment components, permitted variations, error labels, and corrective instructions.
7. **Cultural review:** validate history, terminology, and interpretation.
8. **Technical pilot:** test with partner instructor and beginner learners.
9. **Release sign-off:** partner reviews final public content and confirms the permitted access level.
10. **Ongoing review:** version and periodically revalidate content/rubrics.

## 4. Required partner documentation

- Partner organization identity and authorized representative.
- Instructor/maestro names, preferred attribution, and role.
- Tradition/style and region being represented.
- Permission for each asset (video, photo, audio, movement record, terms, narrative).
- Permission scope: internal research, public educational use, commercial use, derivative use, download rights, and duration.
- Access level: public, community-only, restricted.
- Whether the partner can approve updates or request takedown.
- Revenue/benefit-sharing principles if paid content/services are planned.
- Safety and curriculum approval record.

No single partner can automatically authorize content owned/held by another school, lineage, community, or practitioner.

## 5. Movement validation worksheet

For every movement, create a record with:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Movement ID | Stable identifier, e.g. `MOV-001`. |
| Movement name | Local and Bahasa Indonesia names as approved. |
| Tradition/style | Explicit style/lineage or school context. |
| Source partner | School/instructor and provenance. |
| Cultural context | Meaning/function and any restrictions. |
| Teaching objective | What the beginner is expected to learn. |
| Start/end positions | Instructor-approved descriptions. |
| Phases | Ordered phases with frame/time references. |
| Observable criteria | Only aspects the camera can plausibly assess. |
| Allowed variations | Variations that must not be marked incorrect. |
| Common errors | Instructor-approved error taxonomy. |
| Correction text/audio | Exact instructional cues approved for the lesson. |
| Safety notes | Surface, space, warmup, movement restrictions. |
| Reference files | Permissioned assets and SHA/version. |
| Rubric version | Versioned assessment specification. |
| Review status | Draft, in review, approved, suspended, retired. |
| Access level | Public, community-only, restricted. |

## 6. Consent and access design

- Obtain informed consent before capture or publication.
- Explain intended use in everyday language.
- Allow a knowledge holder to decline parts of a recording.
- Offer a non-public/private recording route where appropriate.
- Respect restricted/sacred material and community governance.
- Provide a documented takedown/restriction procedure.
- Preserve attribution and do not remove partner context from an asset.

## 7. Instructor review workflow

1. Platform editor creates/updates draft.
2. Partner instructor receives a review package with video, movement phases, metadata, proposed criteria, and feedback text.
3. Instructor comments on correctness, permitted variations, and safety.
4. Cultural editor checks context and rights.
5. Technical team updates reference/rubric version.
6. Final preview is reviewed by partner.
7. Approval is recorded with person, date, version, scope, and evidence.
8. Publish only after required approvals.

## 8. Cultural red flags

Do not publish or score when:
- the team cannot identify the source of a movement;
- the movement is restricted/sacred or the permission is unclear;
- two instructors disagree and the difference has not been documented;
- the platform presents one school’s form as the only authentic national form;
- a demonstration video is reused without permission;
- the AI feedback changes the cultural meaning of the movement;
- a movement is unsafe for independent beginner practice.

## 9. Partner benefit model

Potential benefits include attribution, new beginner leads, paid learning content, class registrations, school management tools, documentation support, and a share of revenue generated from the partner's content. The commercial terms must be agreed, transparent, and proportionate to partner contribution.
