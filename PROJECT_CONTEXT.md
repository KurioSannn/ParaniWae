# SILATARA - Project Context & Strategic Brief

**Version:** 1.0  
**As-of date:** 19 November 2026  
**Status:** Product concept / requirements discovery; implementation status must be audited in the target repository.

## 1. Executive summary

SILATARA is a proposed web-based ecosystem for learning and preserving Indonesia's diverse pencak silat traditions. It combines:

1. **Cultural knowledge preservation:** documented traditions, histories, terminology, movement context, instructor attribution, and approved cultural materials.
2. **Structured learning:** beginner-to-advanced learning paths, but the MVP only covers the partner-approved beginner curriculum.
3. **AI-assisted practice:** browser-camera pose and hand tracking, phase/sequence assessment, transparent movement feedback, and optional text-to-speech guidance.
4. **Progress tracking:** personal practice history and measurable development.
5. **Community connection:** a directory of verified partner schools/instructors and a path from independent discovery to in-person learning.
6. **Long-term partner economy:** tools for instructors/schools, paid classes/content where appropriate, institutional licensing, and transparent benefit sharing.

The product should not be framed as an app to become a fighter, beat opponents, or learn violence. It is a culturally grounded learning and preservation platform that promotes discipline, self-control, respect, safety, and intergenerational transmission.

## 3. Cultural foundation

UNESCO inscribed **Traditions of Pencak Silat** on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2019. UNESCO describes sporting, mental-spiritual, self-defence, and artistic dimensions. It also notes regional variation in movement, styles, accompanying music, costumes, instruments, and traditional weapons, and the role of oral traditions, philosophical expressions, advice, songs, and instrument techniques.

This has a direct product implication: SILATARA must not flatten all traditions into one universal pose standard. Every assessed movement must be tied to a named tradition/style and a partner-approved reference. The first release can represent one partner's material accurately; expansion to other traditions happens only after new partners and validators are involved.

## 4. Problem framing

The product hypothesis combines three related problems. These are hypotheses supported by prior desk research and user-research claims from the founder; they must be validated against actual survey/interview files before quantified statements are used.

### 4.1 Knowledge transmission gap

Movement knowledge, local terminology, philosophy, and teaching methods are often transferred through direct practice and teacher-student interaction. A video archive can preserve a recording but does not automatically preserve context, permissible variations, correction methods, or provenance.

### 4.2 Beginner access and confidence gap

Some young people express an interest in learning pencak silat but may not know where to start, may have limited access to a suitable school, or may feel uncomfortable making mistakes in front of peers. A safe, private, beginner-oriented digital entry point may reduce some barriers. This must be tested, not assumed.

### 4.3 Perception and community participation gap

The founder's research hypothesis is that negative public perceptions caused by the conduct of some individuals or groups can affect how newcomers perceive pencak silat. This must be expressed carefully: do not generalize misconduct to all schools, practitioners, or traditions. The platform should foreground positive instruction, cultural context, safety, service, discipline, and community contribution.

## 9. Competitive and academic prior art

The desk research found several relevant approaches:

- motion-capture documentation of Silat Tuo Minangkabau (2018);
- mobile augmented-reality/multimedia pencak silat learning (2022);
- CNN-based movement classification using body pose (ITS, 2023);
- systematic literature review of technology in pencak silat (2024);
- MediaPipe/MLP/CNN-based detection of jurus tunggal poses (2025 thesis);
- MediaPipe-based classification of 62 Ibing Penca stances, reporting 95.2% success in that study (published 30 April 2026);
- a web-based single-artistic-jurus scoring thesis using MediaPipe, joint angles, transition score, and duration (repository record dated 10 October 2026).

Therefore, do not claim that MediaPipe, pose estimation, joint-angle analysis, sequence matching, or web-based pencak silat scoring are globally novel. The proposed differentiated contribution is the **integration** of partner-curated cultural provenance, tradition-specific movement standards, explainable instructor-calibrated coaching, beginner learning journeys, and community/economic pathways. Each novelty claim must be framed relative to the literature actually reviewed.

## 10. Product strategy

### Vision

Make it easier for the next generation to discover, understand, and safely learn pencak silat while preserving the knowledge and livelihoods of its cultural practitioners.

### Product direction update (2026-11-21, owner directive, supersedes the partner-first MVP framing below)
SILATARA is now **public-first**. Source-backed general knowledge (traditions, the national map across 38 provinces,
movements documented from academic and official sources, a public learning curriculum) is integrated into the main
pages and readable without login. A school's official material still requires that school's confirmation, and
private pilot programs stay protected. Camera assessment remains limited to instructor-approved rubrics. See
`CLAUDE.md` and `docs/adr/0008-public-first-knowledge.md`. The "one partner, 8-12 movements" scope below still applies
to **AI assessment**, not to public knowledge pages.

### MVP strategy

Start with one credible partner school/style, not a pan-Indonesian catalogue. Co-design an approved beginner curriculum of approximately 8-12 movements. Build one end-to-end user journey: discover a tradition -> learn a movement -> watch an instructor reference -> practice with a camera -> receive explainable feedback -> track progress -> find a verified partner class.

### First release features

- Heritage Explorer (partner-curated basics and provenance).
- Structured Academy (beginner curriculum and instructor videos).
- AI Practice Coach (pose + hand landmarks, selected safe solo movements, rule-based/expert-calibrated assessment, text/voice feedback).
- Progress tracking.
- Verified partner directory (simple public profile, location entered by partner, contact/class information).

### Later capabilities

- Multi-tradition expansion.
- Partner/maestro portal and content workflow.
- Booking/payments.
- Premium learning content and revenue share.
- Institutional/school packages.
- Larger temporal ML models after data volume/permissions justify them.

## 11. Safety and scope

- MVP is for safe solo beginner practice, not sparring or combat training.
- Exclude partner-contact drills, throws, weapon techniques, high-impact moves, acrobatics, and dangerous techniques from unsupervised practice.
- The app must state that it is not a substitute for an instructor and cannot certify belts/ranks or combat readiness.
- The AI may assess only observable parameters approved by the partner instructor. It cannot reliably infer force, intent, efficacy in combat, or all artistic/cultural dimensions from an ordinary RGB camera.
- Camera use must be opt-in; on-device inference and no raw video persistence by default.

## 12. Success definition

A successful MVP is not “many features”. It is a working, partner-validated vertical slice that:
- teaches a small approved movement set;
- detects the user’s pose reliably enough in defined camera conditions;
- identifies when data quality is too poor to score;
- compares movement against partner-approved standards;
- produces understandable, validated corrections;
- demonstrates measurable learning progress;
- gives users a credible way to continue with real instructors;
- protects the partner’s cultural knowledge, media, consent, and attribution.
